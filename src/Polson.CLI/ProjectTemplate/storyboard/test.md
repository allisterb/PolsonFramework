## What this workflow tests

`storyboard` is the **arranged** route with bought actors — `Scene.createSet` / `createShot` for
continuity, `Assets.cutout` for the cast, and a clock long enough that the choice between buying a
face and constructing one is a real one. Reach for `polson://manual/20`, `polson://manual/21` and
`polson://manual/16`.

Answer these in `findings.md`:

- **Did the cast plan survive contact?** You were told to list every expression before the first
  requisition, because a later one arrives as a different person. Did you get the list right? Which
  expressions did you buy and never use, and which did you need and not have — and what did you do
  about the second?
- **One call per character.** Was that clear from the published resources, or did you have to work out
  what `variants` guarantees? If you put two characters in one call, say what came back.
- **What did `split` report, and did you look?** `'even'` means the sheet was divided into equal
  columns rather than on its gaps, which clips shoulders and renders perfectly. Did you check before
  or after you noticed something wrong?
- **Placing a trimmed cell.** Cells differ in aspect because each is cropped to its own extent. Was
  that a help or a nuisance, and what did you have to compute that the SDK could have given you?
- **Continuity is unchecked.** Nothing in the run will tell you the space held still. Did the
  `createSet` / `createShot` pair make that a non-question, or did you still have to look?
- **The reverse angle.** A crop cannot turn around. Did you meet it, and which way out did you take?
- **Buying versus constructing.** This is the whole premise. For the panels that carried a face:
  would constructing it have been better, and by how much clock? If you constructed one anyway, say
  which panel and why — that is a more useful result than following the instruction.
- **What did you spend?** Requisitions, seconds waited, budget remaining. And whether a flat shape
  would have said the same thing in the panels where you used a face.
