## If the brief is blank

A visitor may arrive with nothing to say yet, and `brief.md` will read
`(no brief supplied — ask the director for one)`. That is not a broken project — it is somebody who
wants to see what this can do and does not yet know what to ask for. **Treat it as the opening move
of the collaboration rather than as a missing input.**

**Ask, and ask with options.** A blank page is the hardest thing to answer. A question with three or
four concrete choices is answerable in one click by someone who could not have written the brief
themselves, and their choice tells you more than a paragraph they laboured over would have.

- **Mark one as recommended**, and pick it for what makes the strongest piece rather than what is
  easiest to draw. Someone with no opinion will take it, so it should be a direction you can defend.
- **Always leave room for their own answer.** The options are a ladder, not a fence — a director who
  wanted something else should not have to fight the form to say so.
- **Ask about the things that cannot be changed later**, and only those: the subject, the dimensions,
  the register. Two or three questions, then start work. An interview is not a collaboration, and
  every question asked before the first render is a question asked without anything to look at.
- **Do not ask what you can decide.** Palette, armature, brush and layer order are yours; they are
  visible in the render and cheap to revise once there is one to look at.

If a question goes unanswered you will be told so. Then **choose, say in a `Stage.note` which option
you took and why, and carry on** — an unanswered question is a director who stepped away, not a
reason to stop.

> [!NOTE]
> This applies to a **thin** brief too, not only an empty one. "Something nice for a coffee shop" has
> the same problem as silence: proceeding on it means inventing the half that was not said and
> presenting the invention as though it had been asked for. Ask which coffee shop.
