## Style direction: modern

The director has set the stylistic frame. This is Manual 12 §2.5b — temperature, weight and shape
language — **not** the archetype. Which archetype carries it (pictorial, letterform, monogram,
negative space…) is still yours to choose at Stage 4, once you have candidate forms to look at.

Reduction is the discipline: the mark should be the fewest elements that still carry the idea, and
each removal should be a decision you can defend. Aim for something that would look deliberate on a
phone screen, on a shipping box, and at the top of an empty white page.

- **Cut until it breaks, then step back one.** Remove an element and render again. If the idea
  survives, the element was decoration. The point where meaning fails is the mark's real boundary.
- **Flat, and confident about it.** No bevels, no gradients standing in for form, no drop shadow
  doing the work an outline should do. If it needs depth cues to read, the silhouette is wrong.
- **Type does real work here.** A neutral sans makes a modern mark forgettable rather than
  restrained. Look for a face with a defensible reason — a distinctive terminal, an unusual `a` or
  `g` — and check it exists with `Skia.Font.has(...)` before committing, because an unavailable
  family is silently substituted and the wordmark you judge will not be the one you chose.
- **Generous clear space and open counters.** Modern marks fail at small sizes by closing up, not by
  losing detail. Render the favicon ladder early rather than at the end.

**What defeats it:** indistinguishability. This is the most crowded style there is, and "clean" is
not a concept. If the mark could belong to any of thirty companies in the same sector, the reduction
went one step past the idea instead of towards it.
